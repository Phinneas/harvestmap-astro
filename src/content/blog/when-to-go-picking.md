The single biggest mistake u-pick beginners make is showing up at the wrong time. Strawberries in June are perfect; strawberries in August are a disappointment. Here's how to time it right.

## The golden rule: call ahead

Every guide, every calendar, every seasonality chart on this site is an estimate. Weather shifts harvest windows by weeks. A warm spring pulls strawberries two weeks early. A late frost kills the peach blossoms. **Always call the farm before you drive.**

## How seasons shift by region

The same crop ripens at different times depending on where you are:

- **Strawberries**: May in the South, June in the Midwest, June–July in the Northeast, March–April in California
- **Blueberries**: June in the South, July in the Midwest and Northeast, July–August in the Pacific Northwest
- **Apples**: August in the South, September–October everywhere else
- **Pumpkins**: Late September through October everywhere

This is why HarvestMap estimates seasonality from your USDA hardiness zone — it's a rough proxy for first-frost date and growing season length.

## The three phases of a harvest

Every crop goes through three phases:

1. **Early season**: Fruit is available but not at peak flavor. Prices may be higher because supply is low. Good if you want to beat the crowds.
2. **Peak season**: This is when you want to go. Fruit is abundant, flavor is best, and farms are fully stocked. Expect crowds on weekends.
3. **Late season**: The tail end. Quality varies — some late varieties are excellent (like late-season apples), but pickings get slim. Prices may drop.

## Weekday vs. weekend

If you have flexibility, go on a weekday morning. Fields are less picked-over, crowds are smaller, and the farmer has time to chat. Weekends are busy, especially in peak season. By Sunday afternoon, the best fruit may be gone.

## What "in season" means on HarvestMap

When you see a farm flagged as "harvesting now" on HarvestMap, it means the crop's estimated harvest window overlaps with the current date, based on the farm's hardiness zone. It's a starting point, not a guarantee. The farm may have finished early, or a late spring may have delayed them.

That's why we built the ["Report what's ripe"](/report) feature — so visitors and growers can confirm what's actually available, not just what's estimated to be.

## Planning your season

Here's a rough calendar for the most popular u-pick crops:

| Month | What's peaking |
|-------|---------------|
| March | Asparagus, citrus |
| April | Asparagus, strawberries (South) |
| May | Strawberries, peas, herbs |
| June | Strawberries, blueberries (South), cherries |
| July | Blueberries, raspberries, peaches, cherries |
| August | Peaches, blackberries, early apples, sweet corn, tomatoes |
| September | Apples, pears, grapes, raspberries (everbearing) |
| October | Apples, pumpkins, winter squash |
| November | Late apples, persimmons, chestnuts |
| December | Citrus (South), Christmas trees |

Use this as a planning guide, then check HarvestMap for farms near you and call to confirm.
