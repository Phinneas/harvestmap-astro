### Commercial Versus Home Freeze Dried

USU Extension states that eating home-frozen-dried products isn't recommended beyond 5 to 10 years after processing, since nutrient and quality loss runs faster than in commercial counterparts. The same source notes that freeze-dried foods can stay safe and stable for 10 to 15 years, and sometimes up to 25 years, if you store them in airtight, moisture-proof packaging with oxygen absorbers and keep them cool, dark and dry.

### What the 25-Year Number Really Means

Treat 25 years as a ceiling, not a promise. The LSU AgCenter, citing the University of Minnesota Extension, describes it as a shelf life under ideal storage conditions. Your packaging, temperature and light exposure decide the real result.

Safety and quality run on different clocks. USU Extension reports that sensitive vitamins such as vitamin C can decline significantly within 1 to 3 years, even while the food stays stable and safe. A jar that tastes flat at year six isn't spoiled. It's just older than its best flavor.

## Options and Methods: Packaging Freeze Dried Food for Storage

Match the container to how long you plan to keep the food. Four tiers cover almost every situation.

### The four packaging tiers

**Resealable jars and containers, short term.** Use airtight jars if you plan to eat the food within a month. At this stage you don't need an oxygen absorber. Humid weather speeds spoilage in a loosely sealed jar, so seal it properly.

**Glass canning jars, medium to long term.** Add an oxygen absorber or pull a vacuum with a lid attachment. Glass blocks light poorly, so store clear jars in a dark pantry, basement, or cellar.

**Mylar bags, long term.** The UC Master Food Preserver program states that "for a 25-year shelf life, Mylar bags are the easiest and least expensive option." Light can't penetrate Mylar, and an oxygen absorber plus an impulse seal creates an airtight barrier. Most Mylar bags run 4 to 7 millimeters thick. The longer you plan to store, the thicker the bag should be. Put filled bags inside a food-safe tote or bucket to stop mice and insects.

**#10 cans, maximum protection.** These need a can sealer, which may be costly, but they block light completely and keep pests out. Add oxygen absorbers.

### Oxygen absorber sizing

Match the absorber to the container:

| Container | Oxygen absorber |
|---|---|
| 1 quart | 100 cc |
| 1 gallon | 1 to 300 cc |
| 2 gallons | 2 at 500 cc or 1000 cc |
| 5 gallons | 3 at 1000 cc |

Keep every container dark, dry, and cool. The University of Arizona Extension puts the optimal storage temperature for freeze dried foods between 32°F and 75°F. Keep containers away from furnaces and stoves. When you open a partial container, reseal or vacuum seal it immediately.

Skip the attic and the garage. Heat exposure compromises quality even when the food stays safe.

## Costs and Trade-Offs: Buying vs. Making vs. Other Preservation Methods

Buying commercial freeze-dried food is the fast route, and you pay for it. Commercial companies often carry a mark-up of up to 85% over home-produced product (Jessen, 2018, cited by UC ANR). That premium buys convenience, tested packaging, and someone else's labor.

Making it yourself shifts the cost to equipment and time. A mid-size machine handles 7 to 10 pounds per batch. With a 24-hour process plus 3-hour defrost, it takes over a week to freeze dry one bushel of peaches (F133, F135).

### Shelf Life and Nutrition Against Canning and Dehydrating

Freeze-dried food reaches up to a 25-year shelf life, while dehydrated food runs 4 months to 1 year (F21). Freeze drying removes up to 98% of water versus 80 to 90% for a dehydrator (F22, F23, F97). On nutrition, freeze drying retains as much as 97% of heat-sensitive vitamins, while canned foods can lose up to 60% and dehydrated foods up to 40% (F173, F174).

Skip the DIY machine unless you will run it constantly. The break-even math only works at volume, and most buyers never reach it.

## FAQ

### How to store freeze dried food long term?

Pack it in an airtight container with an oxygen absorber, because freeze dried food reabsorbs moisture from the air and spoils. The University of California Master Food Preserver program states that "for a 25-year shelf life, Mylar bags are the easiest and least expensive option." Add an oxygen absorber, then seal with an impulse sealer or a hot iron. Mylar blocks light, so place the bags inside a food-safe tote or bucket to keep mice and insects out. Glass canning jars with oxygen absorbers work for medium or long-term storage, but keep clear jars in a dark area.

### Why does freeze dried food last so long?

Freeze drying removes up to 98 percent of the water in the food, which leaves too little moisture for microorganisms to grow and slows enzyme action. The UC Master Food Preserver program notes that a dehydrator removes 80-90 percent of the moisture content, while freeze drying goes further and produces a much lower water activity. With oxygen absorbers and moisture-proof packaging, the University of Minnesota Extension states that commercially prepared freeze-dried foods can have a shelf life of up to 25 years. Nutrient quality still declines over time, so long shelf life isn't the same as unchanged food.

### What are the disadvantages of freeze-dried food?

Cost, nutrient loss over time, and moisture after opening are the real drawbacks. Vitamin C "can decline significantly within 1–3 years," according to Utah State University Extension, even while overall quality stays high. Commercial freeze-dried food comes with a markup, and making your own means buying an expensive machine. Rehydration has its own risk. South Dakota State University Extension warns that "if the freeze-dried product is re-hydrated, there is a risk that any pathogen that was on the product will now have an opportunity to grow, especially if the product is hydrated to where the water activity is >0.85." Store opened food sealed.

### What are the top 10 survival foods to stockpile?

No single official top 10 list exists, so build yours around foods that store well and that you'll actually eat. Freeze-dried fruits, vegetables, cooked meats, eggs, and complete meals rehydrate quickly and hold quality for years. The UC Master Food Preserver program notes that nearly any food can be freeze dried, while fewer foods dehydrate and rehydrate successfully. Pair freeze-dried items with shelf-stable staples and water. Utah State University Extension advises storing in airtight, moisture-proof packaging with oxygen absorbers, kept in cool, dark, and dry conditions.

### Is it safe to store freeze dried food in the attic or garage?

No. Attics and garages swing far outside the temperature range freeze dried food needs, so skip them. University of Arizona Extension states that "the optimal storage temperature for freeze dried foods is between 32º F and 75º F," and that exposure to heat compromises quality. Keep containers away from heat sources such as a furnace or stove. A dark pantry, basement, or cellar holds closer to that range. If your only space is a garage, monitor it and move the food indoors when temperatures climb.
