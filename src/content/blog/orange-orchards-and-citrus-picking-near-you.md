Arizona, Florida and Texas listings are thinner. They're still worth checking before you write off a trip.

Not every area of a state has orange orchards open to the public, though. A state page can look full while your county has nothing within an hour. When the usual answer doesn't apply, widen the radius or check a neighboring state before you give up on the season.

Call before you go. PickYourOwn.org warns that oranges are affected by weather, both rain and cooler temperature, more than most crops. On weekends a large turnout can pick a field clean before noon. Leave early.

My take: treat any listing as a lead, not a reservation. One phone call saves the drive.

## Orange Picking Season by State

Citrus picking runs in winter, which surprises people who expect a summer harvest. Sweet orange trees bloom in early to mid spring, usually April to mid-May. Their fruit then takes seven to 15 months to ripen, according to garden.eco.

![Diagram: Orange Picking Season by State](/diagrams/napkin-da48682730.png)

| State | Peak picking window |
|---|---|
| California | Navel October through June, Valencia March through June, often into fall in warmer groves |
| Arizona | Winter into spring, following the navel and Valencia windows |
| Florida | Winter, with navel early and Valencia later |
| Texas | Winter, Gulf Coast groves leading |

These are the windows the variety research supports, not fixed dates. Navel oranges run October through June. Valencia runs March through June and extends into fall in warmer California groves. Satsuma mandarins are the exception, ripening September through November.

Weather in USDA zones 9 through 11 can shorten or extend a picking season by up to a month. A farm's actual window may sit earlier or later than the table.

### Judging ripeness in the field

UC Davis gives two maturity indices: a soluble solids to acid ratio of 8 or higher with yellow-orange color on at least 25 percent of the fruit surface, or a ratio of 10 or higher with green-yellow color on 25 percent or more.

Color alone misleads. Garden.eco notes that green chlorophyll hides the carotenoids unless the fruit spends enough time below 55°F (12.7°C). A green-shouldered orange can still be sweet, so ask the grower before you walk away from a tree.

## What to Bring and How to Pick

Bring your own shallow containers if you want to skip a fee. Pack water, snacks, a hat, and sunscreen. Some deet is worth adding if it has been rainy.

### Containers

Most growers furnish picking containers, but they may charge for them, so confirm before you go. If you bring your own, keep the fruit shallow. PickYourOwn.org warns that heaping oranges more than 14 inches deep will bruise the fruit on the bottom. Plastic dishpans and metal oven pans with 3 inch tall sides make good containers, according to the same guide. I would skip deep buckets and boxes entirely. The fruit at the bottom pays for the extra capacity.

### After Picking

Spread the fruit out when you get home instead of leaving it piled in the car or on the counter. Piling traps heat and pressure bruises, and citrus bruises quietly.

Storage temperature depends on where the fruit grew. UC Davis reports that some Florida-grown cultivars can be kept at 0-1°C (32-34°F), while Arizona-grown Valencia oranges should be kept at 9°C (48°F). If you don't know the source, a middle refrigerator setting is the safer bet than the coldest shelf.

## How Much to Pick: Yields and Costs

Pick for your project, not for the size of the orchard. The numbers below let you back into a quantity before you drive out.

**Fresh eating and puree.** About 3 medium oranges equal 1 pound, and 1 pound of fresh oranges yields about 3 cups peeled, sliced fruit or 2 cups puree, according to PickYourOwn.org. If you want 6 cups of puree, say, that is 3 pounds, or roughly 9 medium oranges.

**Canning.** Plan on 2 to 2½ pounds of fresh oranges per quart canned (PickYourOwn.org). A canner load of 7 quarts needs about 17½ pounds. A load of 9 pints needs about 11 pounds.

**Bulk buying.** One bushel is 48 to 50 pounds and yields approximately 18 to 25 quart jars (PickYourOwn.org). A low yield usually means the fruit is running large or you're trimming more.

### Retail price reference

Those are national averages, so a local farm price can sit above or below them.

I would skip the frozen comparison when planning a picking trip. You are paying for someone else's processing, not the fruit, and it tells you nothing about what a u-pick pound is worth to you. Want a few oranges for the week? Buy them at the store and save the trip for a canning batch.

## FAQ

### Where are some good orange groves in Arizona?

Arizona's citrus groves sit in the low desert, and the state's own acreage reports show how thin the picking map gets. The 2024 California Citrus Acreage Report is a California document, so I wouldn't read Arizona totals from it. For a trip, start with the county extension office for Maricopa, Pinal or Yuma County and ask which groves still open to the public. Call before you drive. U-pick citrus is seasonal and small, and a grove that welcomed pickers last winter may sell wholesale only this year.

### Are there any orange orchards left in Florida?

Yes, commercial citrus still grows in Florida. The industry has shrunk, though, and many old groves are now housing or pasture. The 2024 California Citrus Acreage Report tracks California only, so I wouldn't quote a Florida acreage figure from it. What matters for a picking trip is that most remaining Florida groves are wholesale operations, not u-pick. A handful of roadside stands and small groves still let visitors pick. Search by county, call the grower directly, and expect the answer to be no more often than yes.

### Where is the best place to buy Florida oranges?

Buy from a Florida grower or a packer that ships, not from a grocery bin labeled "Florida." The state's commercial crop moves through packinghouses, and roadside stands near the groves sell the same fruit with less travel time. If you want fruit shipped, order directly from a grower. I would skip gift-fruit catalogs that charge premium prices for fruit picked weeks earlier. Ask when the fruit was picked and which variety it's. Navel and Valencia are the two names worth knowing.

### What is the best month to pick oranges?

There is no single month, because orange season runs from fall into spring depending on variety and region. Navel oranges are in season from October through June, according to garden.eco. Valencia hangs longest and often runs March through June, and into fall in warmer California groves, per the same source. In the U.S., pickyourown.org puts the peak at late June through July in the South and July and August in the North. Check with the specific farm, since weather shifts the window by up to a month.

### Fruit orchards and prices for those trees?

No directory can give you real prices, and you should be skeptical of any that tries. U-pick farms set their own rates by pound, bag or container, and those rates change with the crop and the year. So call ahead. Ask whether they charge by weight or by container, whether they supply containers, and whether there's an entry fee. If you bring your own, pickyourown.org warns that heaping oranges more than 14 inches deep will bruise the fruit on the bottom.
