Michigan, Minnesota, Wisconsin, Ohio, and Illinois grow a huge share of the country's apples, and their orchards are built for fall visitors — hayrides, cider mills, and rows of trees you can pick yourself. Here is how to time it, what to look for, and how to pick the best fruit.

## When to go

Midwest apple season runs from **mid-August through late October**, with different varieties ripening in waves. Early apples — Paula Red, Gala, and Ginger Gold — are ready by late August. Honeycrisp, the Midwest's rock star, peaks in September. Late keepers like Fuji, Braeburn, and Pink Lady run into October.

Peak picking for the widest selection is **mid-September through early October**. By late October, the early and mid-season trees are picked out and what remains is the late crop — still good, but less variety. Weekend orchards in west Michigan and along the Minnesota-Wisconsin border get crowded by mid-morning, so go early or on a weekday.

## The big Midwest regions

**West Michigan** is the heart of it — the strip of orchards from Grand Rapids up to Traverse City. Lake Michigan moderates the climate and feeds the state's famous fruit belt. This is where a lot of the country's Honeycrisp and Gala come from.

**The Minnesota–Wisconsin border** (think the St. Croix valley and the bluffs around La Crosse) is another cluster, with orchards that ripen a touch later than Michigan because the climate is colder.

**Ohio and Illinois** orchards spread from the Lake Erie shore down through central Ohio and the orchards around the Shawnee Hills in southern Illinois, where the season starts a little earlier.

## Varieties to look for

**Honeycrisp** is the one everyone asks for — crisp, juicy, and sweet-tart. It is worth timing a trip around. Trees get picked fast, so go in September.

**Gala and Fuji** are sweet and mild, good for eating out of hand and for kids.

**Cortland and McIntosh** are the old-school baking and sauce apples. Cortland holds its shape in a pie and does not brown as fast when sliced.

**Golden Delicious** is the workhorse — sweet, tender, and good for both eating and baking. Do not let the name fool you; a tree-ripened Golden is nothing like the waxy grocery store version.

**Honeygold and Haralson** are Minnesota-bred cold-hardy apples. Haralson is a classic tart pie apple that keeps well into winter.

## How to pick

Ripe apples come off the tree with a **quarter turn and a lift** — if you have to yank, it is not ready. The apple should separate from the spur cleanly, leaving the stem on the fruit. Do not shake branches or pull hard; you will knock down unripe apples and damage next year's spurs.

Reach for fruit on the **outside of the tree** first — it ripens first because it gets the most sun. Leave the stem on, and set apples gently in your bag rather than dropping them; bruises show up a day later.

Most orchards charge by the bag or by the pound. A peck is about 10-12 pounds, a half-bushel around 20-25. That is more apples than most families eat in a week, so plan a pie, applesauce, or a batch of cider.

## What to bring

**Layers and boots.** October weather in the Midwest swings from 70 to 40 degrees, and orchard rows are grass and mud.

**A wagon.** Many orchards lend them, but they run out on busy weekends.

**Cash.** Some small farm stands are still cash-only.

**A plan for the apples.** The best trips end with a baking project. Bring a pie recipe and buy the right variety for it — a mix of tart and sweet apples makes the best filling.
