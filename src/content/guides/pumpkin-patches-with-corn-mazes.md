The best fall farms do double duty: a pumpkin patch for the photo and the porch, and a corn maze for the wandering. Pairing the two turns a quick errand into a full day out. The trick is in the order, the timing, and not underestimating how long a "short" maze actually takes.

## Do the maze first, pumpkins second

If you are doing both, **hit the corn maze first**. Corn mazes need daylight — most close at dusk, and the ones that stay open after dark are a different, spookier experience. Pumpkins, by contrast, sit in the field all day and do not care when you come for them.

The other reason is practical: pumpkins are heavy. You do not want to drag a 15-pound carving pumpkin through a half-mile of corn. Pick the pumpkin last, load it in the car, and head home.

## Time it right

A family corn maze runs 20-30 minutes. A big one can take an hour or more, and a night maze even longer. Budget **two to three hours** for the full experience — maze, patch, a cider donut, and a hayride if the farm offers one.

Go early. Farms with both a patch and a maze are the busiest on October weekends, and the crowds peak between 11 a.m. and 3 p.m. Arrive at opening and you will have the maze mostly to yourself; arrive at noon and you will share every wrong turn with a stroller.

## What to bring for both

**Sturdy shoes.** Corn maze paths are dirt with leftover stalks, and pumpkin patches are worse after rain. Boots cover both.

**A wagon.** For the pumpkin. The farm usually lends them, but they run out.

**Layers.** An October afternoon that starts at 60 degrees can end at 40 once the sun drops. If you are staying for a night maze, bring a real jacket.

**Two bags.** One for the apple cider donuts and the maple syrup from the farm stand, one for the pumpkin. Pumpkins are filthy.

**Cash.** Some farm stands and smaller patches are still cash-only.

## Which pumpkin pairs with a maze

If the pumpkin is for **carving**, grab a Howden — the classic orange globe with thick walls. If it is for **baking**, look for a Sugar Pie; carving pumpkins are watery and bland inside. And if it is for the **photo**, grab a white Lumina or a pile of mini pumpkins, which last for months.

Cut the pumpkin from the vine with shears, leaving 3-4 inches of stem, and carry it from the bottom. A snapped stem shortens its life by weeks.

## After the maze

Once you are out — and you will get out, even if it takes a lap around the perimeter — the farm usually has more: a hayride, a petting area, a cider mill, a pile of pumpkins staged for photos. This is where the afternoon slows down and the day pays off. Grab a cider and a donut, and do not feel bad about taking the "cheat" exit if the kids are done.
