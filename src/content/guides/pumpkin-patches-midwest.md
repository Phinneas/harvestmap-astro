Ohio, Illinois, Indiana, and Michigan are pumpkin country — flat, fertile land and a long cool autumn that turns every farm into a fall destination. Here is how to find the right patch, pick the right pumpkin, and get it home in one piece.

## When to go

Midwest pumpkin season runs from **mid-September through Halloween**, with the peak in the first three weeks of October. Patches open by late September, and the best selection — the big carving pumpkins and the specialty varieties — goes first.

If you want a specific pumpkin — a giant, a white Lumina, a warty Knucklehead — go in **early October**. By the last week before Halloween, the big ones are picked over. And if the weather has been wet, expect mud; the Midwest gets real October rain, and patches are dirt.

## Choosing a patch

Midwest pumpkin operations fall into two camps.

**The u-pick patch** is what most people picture: walk the field, cut your own pumpkin off the vine. Not every farm offers this — some sell pre-picked pumpkins from a bin out front. If walking the field matters, confirm before you go.

**The destination farm** layers the pumpkin patch with corn mazes, hayrides, apple orchards, cider donuts, and a farm store. These are the ones that book up on October weekends — some require timed tickets. If you are making a day of it, reserve ahead.

## Varieties to look for

**Howden** is the classic Midwest carving pumpkin — deep orange, ribbed, thick-walled, and built for a jack-o'-lantern face.

**Connecticut Field** is the old heirloom — large and slightly flat, one of the oldest American pumpkins still grown.

**Sugar Pie** (sometimes called New England Pie) is the baking pumpkin. Small, dense, and sweet. If you want to cook, this is the one; carving pumpkins are watery and bland inside.

**Lumina** is the white ghost pumpkin. Striking on a porch and still carvable.

**Big Max and Atlantic Giant** are the show pumpkins — the ones that weigh hundreds of pounds. Fun to look at, terrible to carry, and usually sold by the farm rather than picked.

**Mini pumpkins** (Jack-Be-Little, Wee-B-Little) are palm-sized and last for months on a table.

## How to pick

A ripe pumpkin has a **hard rind and a dry stem**. Press your thumbnail into the skin — if it dents, it is not ready. The stem should be brown and firm, not green and bendy.

Cut the pumpkin from the vine with shears, leaving **3-4 inches of stem**. Carry it from the bottom, never by the stem — a snapped stem shortens the pumpkin's life. Look for a pumpkin with no soft spots, cuts, or bruises. A flat spot on the bottom is fine and actually helps it sit still for carving.

## Getting it home

Pumpkins are heavy and dirty. Bring a wagon or use the farm's, and bring a tarp for the trunk. An uncarved pumpkin stored in a cool, dry spot — a porch or garage — lasts for weeks, even into Thanksgiving. Once carved, it is a race against the clock: 3 to 7 days, less if it is warm. Carve close to Halloween if you want it to look good on the night.
