Berries are the shortest, sweetest stretch of the u-pick calendar. Strawberries
open the season in spring, blueberries and raspberries carry the summer, and
blackberries close it out. This guide covers berry picking farms near you —
which berry ripens when and where, roughly what you'll pay, and what to bring.
The windows below are the 2027 season; confirm with the farm before you drive,
since weather moves every year.

## Berry picking farms near you

## How to find the best berry farm near you

There's no single "best" berry farm on a national list — the best one is the
one close to you that grows the berry you want, at the right time. Three
checks separate a good u-pick from a frustrating trip:

1. **It's actually in season.** A farm can list blueberries and still be picked
   out this week. Call ahead or check the listing before you drive.
2. **It's u-pick, not just a stand.** Some berry farms sell at the counter but
   don't open rows to the public.
3. **It fits your group.** Kids, dogs, accessibility — filter for what you need
   in the [finder](/u-pick-farms).

For a head start, browse [all berries](/crops/berries) or jump to your
[state's farm directory](/u-pick-farms).

## What berry picking costs

Most u-pick berry farms price by the pound — roughly $3–7 for blueberries,
$2–4 for in-season strawberries, depending on region — and that's still cheaper
than grocery-store organic. Many charge no admission and just weigh what you
pick; a few charge a small entry or a per-person minimum. Filter for
[free admission](/u-pick-farms) to see no-fee farms, and always confirm the
current price when you call, since it moves with the season.

## Free public berry picking

A few places let you pick for free. [Charlotte's Blueberry Park](/farms/wa/charlottes-blueberry-park-402-e-d-street)
in Tacoma, Washington is a public park with blueberry bushes anyone can pick at
no charge — a rare find, and one worth knowing if you're in the Puget Sound
area. These spots are uncommon, so when you find one, treat it gently and leave
berries for the next picker.

## What to bring, and how to pick

**Bring:** cash (many berry farms are card-optional), your own containers if
the farm allows them, sunscreen and a hat (rows have no shade), closed shoes,
and a cooler in the car so berries survive the drive home.

**How to pick:**

- Pick in the morning — cooler fruit, firmer berries, fewer crowds.
- Only take fully colored berries; they don't sweeten after picking.
- Roll a berry gently between your fingers instead of yanking — ripe ones come
  off with almost no pressure.
- Leave strawberry hulls on until you're ready to eat them; they keep longer.
- Check the underside of the plant — the best berries hide under the leaves.

## When not to pick

**When not to pick blackberries:** skip them right after heavy rain, when
they're waterlogged and bland, and never pick the dull, overripe ones — they'll
turn to mush in the bucket. Leave the berries low on the bush near roadsides,
where exhaust and spray settle. And don't pick anywhere you can't identify the
plant; wild lookalikes aren't always edible.

Generally, pass on any berry patch that's muddy, picked clean, or where the
fruit is soft and sunken — it's a sign you've missed the window.

## Frequently asked questions

**What season is best for berry picking?**
Late spring through late summer, depending on the berry. Strawberries peak
April–June, blueberries June–August, and blackberries July–September. See the
table above for your state.

**Which US states are best for berry picking?**
Oregon, Washington, and Michigan lead on blueberries; California leads on
strawberries; and the Northeast and Southeast have deep u-pick traditions. But
the best state for you is the one with farms within driving distance — start
with the [finder](/u-pick-farms).

**What is the best month to go strawberry picking?**
May in most of the country — April across the South and California, June in the
northern tier. Strawberries are the first berry of the year.

**Where can I pick blueberries in Michigan?**
Michigan is one of the country's biggest blueberry states. Browse
[blueberry farms](/crops/blueberries) and the [Michigan directory](/mi), or set
the finder to Blueberries near your Michigan ZIP.

**What should I expect at a berry picking farm?**
You usually pay by the pound or the bucket, rows are marked by variety, and
you'll walk on uneven ground for an hour or two. The farm provides containers
or lets you bring your own.

**Can kids pick berries?**
Usually, yes — low bushes and soft fruit make berries one of the easier
pick-your-own crops for kids. See our guide to
[bringing kids picking](/blog/bringing-kids-picking).
