Loudoun County is Washington, D.C.'s farm country. Forty miles west of the capital, the suburbs give way to rolling Piedmont hills, horse farms, and one of the densest concentrations of small farms in the Mid-Atlantic. In fall, the county transforms: apple orchards open for picking, pumpkin patches fill the fields, and the farm wineries that made Loudoun famous add harvest events to their tasting menus.

This route runs through western Loudoun County, from the Blue Ridge foothills east to the villages. It works any weekend from late September through late October. The season runs a bit longer here than in the Northeast — the Virginia climate extends the window.

## The route

### Stop 1 — Blue Ridge foothills orchard (9:00 – 10:30 AM)

Start in the foothills west of Leesburg, where the land rises toward the Blue Ridge. The elevation keeps the air cool and the apples crisp — the same terroir that makes Virginia's mountain apple country famous. These orchards are smaller and less commercialized than the big destination farms closer to D.C., which means shorter lines and a more personal experience.

**What is ripe in late September:** Gala, Honeycrisp, Fuji, Golden Delicious. By mid-October, add Red Delicious, Rome, and Granny Smith. Virginia's warmer climate means some varieties ripen a week or two earlier than in the Northeast.

**What to bring:** Comfortable shoes (the terrain is rolling, not flat), layers (Virginia October mornings are cool but afternoons can be warm), and a bag. Some orchards charge by the bag; others by the pound.

### Stop 2 — Farm winery with a farm store (11:00 AM – 12:00 PM)

Loudoun County has over 40 wineries, and many of them are working farms. The best ones for this itinerary grow their own fruit — grapes, apples, pears — and operate farm stores alongside the tasting room. You are not just tasting wine; you are visiting a farm.

Pick up:
- **Virginia wine** — the state's signature varietals include Viognier, Petit Verdot, and Cabernet Franc. Ask what pairs with fall produce.
- **Local cheese** — several Loudoun creameries make excellent aged cheddar and fresh chèvre.
- **Apple butter and preserves** — made from local fruit, sold at farm stores.
- **Honey** — Loudoun's beekeepers produce wildflower and clover honey from the surrounding meadows.

### Stop 3 — Pumpkin patch and corn maze (12:30 – 2:00 PM)

Loudoun's farms do fall big. The pumpkin patches here often include corn mazes, hayrides, bounce pillows, and family activities. This is the stop where you spend the most time — budget at least 90 minutes.

**The corn maze** is worth doing. Loudoun's mazes range from simple 20-minute walks to complex 45-minute puzzles. Some farms design new themes each year.

**The pumpkin patch** has the usual range — Howden for carving, Sugar Pie for baking, decorative gourds for the porch. Virginia's longer growing season means pumpkins are often larger here than in the Northeast.

### Stop 4 — Village farmers' market (2:30 – 3:30 PM)

End in Leesburg or Middleburg at the Saturday market. Loudoun's markets are well-attended and well-curated — you will find produce, baked goods, Virginia peanuts, artisan products, and crafts from the surrounding farms.

The markets are also the best place to:
- **Talk to growers** about what is coming ripe next week.
- **Stock up on staples** — eggs, bread, cheese, honey — that you cannot get at the orchards.
- **Find specialty items** — fermented foods, hot sauce, herbal teas, and handmade soaps.

## Timing notes

- **Late September** is the best window: the weather is ideal (60s and 70s), the apple variety is wide, and the crowds have not peaked.
- **Early to mid-October** is peak season. Loudoun's proximity to D.C. means popular farms get crowded on October weekends. Arrive at opening or visit on a weekday.
- **Late October** is still good — the apple late-season varieties are going, and the pumpkin patches stay open through Halloween.
- **Virginia's climate is milder than the Northeast.** Mornings are cool but rarely freezing. Afternoons in October can be warm enough for short sleeves.

## Practical tips

- **Book ahead for popular farms.** Some Loudoun orchards and corn mazes require weekend reservations in October. Check websites before you drive out.
- **The wineries get busy in the afternoon.** If wine tasting is a priority, do that stop early (before noon) or save it for the end of the day when the morning crowd has cleared.
- **Traffic on Route 15 and Route 7 can be heavy on October weekends.** Budget extra time for the drive between stops, especially in the afternoon.
- **Bring a cooler.** You will buy more than you expect — cheese, cider, baked goods, and produce all benefit from a cool car.
